## Why this approach

WW currently transfers the original seated man's bone rotations (`source.rotation * retargetDelta`) and hip position, then applies SeatDrop. Different arm lengths and the lowered hips can leave the replacement wrists away from the wheel even when those rotations look reasonable. This is a code-level explanation to investigate, not a reproduced diagnosis of WW's current visual defect. Measure wrist/target error before changing it.

WWW instead solves each arm to an absolute world-space wrist target attached to the rotating wheel. Its static seated arm pose supplies a stable elbow bend direction.

## Implementation recipe

1. **Cache wheel and arm references on attach.** Find the actual rotating steering-wheel transform, its center, radius and column axis in WW; log its path/frame once. `hinge_steeringwheel` is an installation hinge used by WW and is not proof that this is the rotating visual. WWW's wheel plane is XZ, local +Y points toward the driver, and rotation is around Y. Adapt WW's observed geometry to that convention rather than assuming its asset axes match.

2. **Create two wheel-relative grip frames**, at 9 and 3 o'clock. In WWW wheel coordinates their positions are `(-radius,0,0)` and `(radius,0,0)`. Left fingers point +X toward the hub, right fingers -X. Rotations are `LookRotation(right, Cross(right, forward))` and `LookRotation(left, Cross(left, forward))`. Both grips must follow the actual wheel rotation, not just the car root. WWW uses 95% of the model's measured rim radius; fit WW's own geometry.

3. **Calibrate hand orientation from the rig once**, before applying wheel IK. For each hand, with wrist `h`, middle knuckle `m`, index knuckle `i`, little knuckle `l`:

   ```csharp
   Vector3 dir = m.position - h.position;
   Vector3 across = l.position - i.position;
   Quaternion frame = Quaternion.LookRotation(dir, Vector3.Cross(dir, across));
   Quaternion handOffset = Quaternion.Inverse(frame) * h.rotation;
   float palmLength = dir.magnitude;
   ```

   Missing finger bones: WWW uses a 0.09 m palm-length fallback when the middle knuckle is absent, and preserves the reset hand's rotation when the full finger frame is unavailable. Do not fabricate finger measurements or copy rig-specific wrist Euler angles.

4. **Solve after all pose and transform changes.** WWW resets upper arm, forearm and hand to cached seated local rotations every frame before solving; otherwise elbow swing can accumulate. WW already reapplies `CarSeat.Pose` each frame, so use that fresh pose as the starting point. Apply final IK after SeatDrop, `LateCar`'s pose fade, `ShiftFirstPerson`, and the wheel's visual rotation. An IK call only inside `CarSeat.Pose` would be overwritten or displaced by later work. Confirm WW's scheduler order before choosing the final callback.

   ```csharp
   Vector3 wrist = grip.position
       - grip.forward * (palmLength * gripReach)
       + columnTowardDriver * gripLift;
   Quaternion wristRotation = hasFingerFrame
       ? grip.rotation * handOffset : resetHand.rotation;
   // Two-bone Reach, full weight; left elbow +swing, right elbow -swing.
   ```

   WWW defaults: `gripReach = 0.85`, `gripLift = 0.03` metres, elbow swing `0` degrees. Port `ArmIK.Solve`'s length clamp (`abs(a-b)+0.001` to `a+b-0.001`), projected current elbow direction, law-of-cosines elbow position, shoulder/forearm rotations, then set wrist rotation. Never stretch bones or move the hand position independently.

5. **Preserve WW's arm ownership.** Enable wheel IK for the normal empty-handed driving pose. Bypass it while guns, reloads, throws or other existing actions own the arms; `LateCarInner` already distinguishes `gun`, `throwing` and `arms`. Preserve existing lance and binocular behavior. WW's first-person arms mesh shares the body's bones, so the same solve should serve both views. Clear references and destroy created grips on detach/car change/module shutdown; retain existing missing-seat fallback. Cache all searches and allocate nothing per frame.
